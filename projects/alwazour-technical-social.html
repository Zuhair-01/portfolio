CLIENT WORK · CONTENT SYSTEM, NOT A ONE-OFF

Rebuilding a technical brand's Instagram

A Damascus cables / networking / CCTV supplier had shipped two how-to carousels. The client rejected both, twice in one day. Instead of a third attempt, I built a reusable visual system: five slide archetypes, a three-layer test, and a one-page QA checklist the client's team now reuses.

The client is Alwazour, the same business whose storefront is in the Live Products section. This case study covers the Instagram content workstream: the brief, the failure, the system, and the measurable difference.

01Problem

The copy was correct. Every spec was verified against the live catalogue. The client still called it "basic, just texts and pasted pictures." Twice in one day.

مين بيعرف شو هالأرقام؟

"who knows what these numbers are" · on a distance-comparison slide

Concretely: whole slides were text on a flat colour field with no visual anchor. A T568A-vs-T568B comparison table had identical text in both columns. Where a photo existed it read as a rectangle dropped on the slide, and the same product shot appeared on three slides; numbers floated with no physical reference.

02Why it matters

The client's own positioning is "specs written as they are, not guessed." For a technical supplier, the Instagram has to visibly demonstrate that competence, not just carry accurate text. Content that looks like a Canva template undercuts the one thing the brand sells: knowing the part well enough to tell you how to use it.

03Approach

Rather than a third solo pass, I ran a structured art-direction consult against real technical-brand references (Fluke Networks, Panduit, Ubiquiti) and the client's live catalogue, then distilled it into a spec. The reframe that drove everything:

Stop designing information slides. Design technical explanations. Every slide carries three layers, and fails review if any is missing:

OBJECT a real product, port, connector or installation INFORMATION one thing the viewer learns by looking at that object STRUCTURE measurement lines, labels, crops, comparison axes keyed to the object. the graphic explains the photo, it is not decoration next to it

A second rule closed the "invented diagram" gap: any technical figure is checked against the real spec sheet — pin order, port count, signal direction. If it can't be verified, it doesn't ship.

04The system

The deliverable is a template system, not two carousels. Five archetypes rotate across any product line: extenders, keystones, PDUs, switches, cable.

Type AHero productCut-out at 55–65% of the canvas, breaking the grid, 1–3 technical callouts. Never a rectangle.
Type BComparisonShow the actual difference (the real pin sequence, the real port layout) and highlight what changes.
Type CHow it worksOne physical product plus a real signal-flow diagram. The system is the visual.
Type DData / scaleA real ruler with product images at each stop. The answer to "who knows what these numbers are."
Type EDetail / macroA macro crop of the real termination or connector, with an annotation chain. The image is the explanation.
Every number gets a noun.120m alone fails; 120m on a physical scale passes.
One exact photo, one slide.A product recurs only when its visual role changes: hero, then macro, then installed.
No AI-drawn hardware.Real photography plus hand-built SVG overlays. Generators invent ports and pinouts.
No slide is 100% text.If the content is a list, that is the case that needs a real graphic, not an excuse to skip one.

07What shipped

  • Five reusable slide archetypes, documented with a CSS spec.
  • A three-layer test (object · information · structure) every slide passes before render.
  • 15 slides rebuilt across two carousels, every one spec-verified.
  • A one-page build checklist the client's team reuses for the next product line. The next carousel does not need a consult.

08Honest limitations

  • Photography is sourced and composited, not shot in-house. A proper product-photo session per SKU would raise the ceiling further.
  • A few slides still lean text-heavy in the CTA and decision archetypes; the visual anchor is present but not dominant.
  • Nothing is posted yet. The client owns the publish decision; this covers design and system only.
